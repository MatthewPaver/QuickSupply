## 1. Industry Overview

### Market Size and Spend

The UK supply teaching market is substantial and growing. In the 2023/24 academic year, schools in England spent **£757.6 million on supply teachers**, of which **£521.9 million** (69%) was channelled through staffing agencies — up 7.5% year-on-year. Per-pupil spend on supply staff reached £929 in special schools, £199 in primary schools, and £188 in secondary schools.

The market is driven by a structural teacher shortage: unfilled vacancy rates are **six times higher than pre-pandemic levels**. Government forecasts project that by 2027/28, England will need 1,600 additional secondary teachers and 12,400 more further education teachers to meet demand. The number of pupils with SEND (Special Educational Needs and Disabilities) has reached 1.7 million, further increasing demand for skilled supply staff.

### Key Market Players — Agencies

The supply teaching market is dominated by large national recruitment agencies:

| Agency | Profile |
|--------|---------|
| **Hays Education** | Part of global Hays plc. One of the largest education recruiters in the UK, covering supply, long-term, and permanent roles. REC Audited Education status holder. |
| **Reed Education** | Works with over 10% of UK schools. Access to nearly one million teaching professionals across all key stages and SEND settings. |
| **Protocol Education** | One of the most recognised supply agencies in the UK. Covers primary, secondary, and SEN schools for both short-term and long-term assignments. |
| **Randstad Education** | Part of global Randstad group. REC Audited Education status. Strong presence in London and South East. |
| **Teaching Personnel** | Large national agency with strong primary school focus. |
| **Capita Education** | REC Audited Education status. Covers both teaching and non-teaching roles. |

These agencies, along with hundreds of smaller regional players, operate within government supply staff frameworks including the Crown Commercial Service (CCS) Supply Teachers and Temporary Staff Framework.

### Market Dynamics

- **Agency dominance**: Approximately 69% of supply spend flows through agencies, creating a persistent margin layer (typically 15-30%) between what schools pay and what teachers receive.
- **Fragmentation**: Schools often work with 3-5 agencies simultaneously, creating administrative overhead.
- **Regional variation**: The South of England has the most severe imbalance between vacancies and available supply teachers, with 8 of the top 10 most-affected areas located in the South.
- **Growing SEND demand**: Special schools and mainstream high-needs units increasingly need specialist supply staff.

## 2. Domain Terminology

### Staff Roles

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Supply Teacher** | A qualified teacher who works on a temporary/day-to-day or short-term basis, covering for absent permanent staff. Paid daily rates derived from the STPCD pay scales (annual salary / 195 teaching days). |
| **Cover Supervisor** | An unqualified member of staff who supervises classes completing pre-set work when a teacher is absent. Cannot deliver new teaching. Lower daily rate than supply teachers. |
| **Teaching Assistant (TA)** | A member of support staff who assists the class teacher. Roles range from general classroom support to 1:1 SEN support. Can be supplied through agencies. |
| **Higher Level Teaching Assistant (HLTA)** | A TA with additional qualifications and responsibilities, able to plan and deliver lessons under the direction of a teacher. |
| **NQT / ECT** | Newly Qualified Teacher (legacy term) / Early Career Teacher (current term from Sept 2021). Teachers in their first two years of teaching, completing a structured induction programme. Supply agencies can place ECTs. |

### Compliance and Safeguarding

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **DBS Check** | Disclosure and Barring Service check. A criminal record check required for anyone working with children. |
| **Enhanced DBS** | The level of DBS check required for school staff — includes spent and unspent convictions, cautions, and police intelligence. Most school roles also require a **barred list check**. |
| **DBS Update Service** | A subscription service (£13/year) allowing DBS certificates to be checked online, avoiding the need for a new full check with each new employer/agency. |
| **Safeguarding** | The actions taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm. All school staff (including supply) must receive safeguarding training. |
| **Single Central Record (SCR)** | A register maintained by every school that records all recruitment checks (DBS, identity, qualifications, right to work, references) for every member of staff, including supply staff provided by agencies. |
| **Right to Work** | Legal requirement to verify that an individual has the right to work in the UK before they start employment. Must be checked by either the agency or the school. |
| **KCSIE** | Keeping Children Safe in Education — the statutory guidance issued by the DfE that schools must follow. Updated annually (latest: September 2025). All staff must read at least Part 1. |
| **Section 128 Check** | A check against the DfE's list of individuals barred from management roles in schools. Required for governors and senior leaders. |

### Education Structure

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **EYFS** | Early Years Foundation Stage. Ages 0-5 (Reception class and below). Has its own curriculum framework and staffing ratios. |
| **Key Stage 1 (KS1)** | Years 1-2 (ages 5-7). |
| **Key Stage 2 (KS2)** | Years 3-6 (ages 7-11). |
| **Key Stage 3 (KS3)** | Years 7-9 (ages 11-14). Secondary. |
| **Key Stage 4 (KS4)** | Years 10-11 (ages 14-16). GCSE years. Secondary. |
| **MAT** | Multi-Academy Trust. A group of academies (schools) under a single trust. Often centralise supply staff procurement. |
| **LA** | Local Authority. Maintains oversight of maintained schools (non-academy). May operate preferred supplier lists for supply agencies. |

### Employment and Pay

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **STPCD** | School Teachers' Pay and Conditions Document. The statutory framework setting pay scales for teachers. Supply rates are derived from this (annual salary / 195 days). |
| **AWR** | Agency Workers Regulations 2010. After 12 continuous weeks in the same role at the same school, supply staff gain the right to pay parity with directly employed staff. |
| **12-Week Rule** | The qualifying period under AWR. Counts calendar weeks (any time worked in a week counts). School holidays pause but do not reset the clock unless the break exceeds 6 weeks. |
| **Umbrella Company** | An intermediary that employs supply teachers on behalf of agencies. Handles PAYE, NIC, pension auto-enrolment, and employment rights. From April 2026, new legislation makes the supplying agency jointly liable for PAYE compliance. |
| **PAYE** | Pay As You Earn. The standard UK tax collection system for employees. Supply teachers paid via umbrella companies or directly by agencies are paid through PAYE. |
| **Finders Fee / Temp-to-Perm Fee** | A fee charged by agencies when a school hires a supply teacher permanently. Typically calculated as a percentage of annual salary, reducing over time. |

## 3. Regulatory Requirements

### DBS and Vetting

All individuals working in schools in regulated activity must have an **Enhanced DBS check with barred list information** before commencing work. For supply staff provided by agencies:

- The **agency is responsible** for obtaining the enhanced DBS certificate before the person begins work at any school.
- The agency must provide **written confirmation** to the school that all required checks (DBS, barred list, identity, qualifications, right to work, prohibition checks) have been completed.
- The **school must verify identity** — confirming the person presenting themselves is the same person on whom checks were conducted.
- Supply staff must be recorded on the school's **Single Central Record**.
- Schools should use the **DBS Update Service** to perform status checks on supply staff certificates where possible.

### Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE 2025)

KCSIE is the statutory safeguarding guidance for schools and colleges, updated annually. Key requirements for supply staff:

1. **Part 1 briefing**: All supply staff must receive a safeguarding briefing including the school's safeguarding policy, the identity of the Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL), and procedures for reporting concerns.
2. **Agency assurance**: Schools must obtain written confirmation from agencies that all pre-employment checks have been completed to the same standard the school would apply.
3. **Proportionate information sharing**: Schools take a risk-based approach to the level of safeguarding information shared with temporary staff, but core procedures must always be communicated.
4. **Online safety**: Supply staff should be aware of the school's acceptable use policy for technology.
5. **Allegations management**: Procedures must cover supply staff — the school manages any allegation investigation even though the agency is the employer.

### Agency Workers Regulations (AWR) 2010

- From **Day 1**: Supply workers are entitled to access to shared facilities (staff room, parking, canteen) and information about permanent vacancies.
- After **12 continuous weeks** in the same role at the same hirer: entitlement to the same basic pay and conditions as a directly recruited employee (pay parity).
- **Anti-avoidance**: Fines of up to £20,000 for deliberately structuring arrangements to avoid AWR pay parity obligations.
- School holidays pause but do not reset the qualifying period (unless the gap exceeds 6 weeks).

### Right to Work

Employers (including agencies) must verify right to work in the UK before employment begins. This involves checking original documents (passport, visa, share code) and retaining copies. Since 2022, online right to work checks via the Home Office share code system are mandatory for individuals with biometric residence permits/cards.

### Ofsted Expectations

Ofsted inspectors assess safeguarding as part of every inspection. Schools must demonstrate:
- Robust procedures for checking supply staff credentials
- That supply staff understand the school's safeguarding procedures
- That the Single Central Record is complete and up to date, including supply staff entries
- Effective oversight of agency vetting processes

### Umbrella Company Regulation (From April 2026)

New PAYE rules from 6 April 2026 make the **end-client agency** (the one supplying the worker to the school) legally responsible for operating PAYE on the worker's pay. This creates joint and several liability across the supply chain for correct PAYE/NIC compliance, significantly tightening regulation of umbrella companies.

## 4. Workflow Patterns

### Current Booking Workflow (Traditional Agency Model)

The typical supply teacher booking process is manually intensive:

1. **Absence notification** — A teacher calls in sick, often at 6:30-7:00 AM. The school office manager or cover coordinator is notified.
2. **Internal cover check** — The school first checks if internal staff (HLTAs, PPA cover staff) can cover the absence.
3. **Agency contact** — If no internal cover, the school contacts their preferred agencies (typically 2-5) via phone or email. Some schools use WhatsApp groups with agency consultants.
4. **Matching** — Agency consultants manually search their databases or contact supply teachers from their phone lists. Matching is based on availability, DBS status, key stage experience, location/travel distance, and school preference/history.
5. **Confirmation** — Once a match is found, the agency confirms with both school and teacher, usually by phone/text.
6. **Day-of verification** — The supply teacher arrives; the school checks their ID against the DBS certificate and issues a safeguarding briefing pack.
7. **Timesheet/sign-off** — At end of day, the teacher signs a timesheet (often paper-based) which the school countersigns.
8. **Invoicing** — The agency invoices the school (weekly or monthly). The agency pays the teacher via PAYE or umbrella company.

### Pain Points in Current Workflows

| Pain Point | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Last-minute requests** | The majority of daily supply bookings are made before 8:00 AM for same-day cover. Schools may post emergency cover at 8:00 AM needing someone by 8:30 AM. The window for matching is extremely tight. |
| **Phone tag** | Agency consultants make dozens of calls each morning to find available teachers. Teachers may be called by multiple agencies simultaneously, leading to confusion and double-bookings. |
| **No-shows** | Supply teachers accepting a booking but not showing up is a significant problem, leaving schools scrambling for last-minute alternatives. |
| **Quality uncertainty** | Schools cannot easily verify a supply teacher's quality or suitability beyond basic compliance checks. Feedback loops are weak. |
| **Manual record-keeping** | Paper timesheets, spreadsheets for tracking spend, and manual SCR updates create administrative burden. |
| **Cost opacity** | Schools often lack visibility into agency margins or the ability to easily compare rates across agencies. |
| **WhatsApp chaos** | Many agency consultants and schools have adopted WhatsApp for speed, but this creates unstructured, unauditable communication chains. |
| **Geographic mismatch** | Agencies may send teachers who live far from the school, leading to late arrivals or cancellations due to travel issues. |
| **Compliance gaps** | Keeping track of DBS expiry dates, update service checks, and safeguarding training across a pool of supply staff is error-prone when done manually. |

### Existing Technology Solutions

| Platform | Model | Key Features |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Teacher Booker** (IRIS) | Agency-managed booking platform | Booking wizard, supply pool management, rate controls, spend reporting. Schools control which agencies and at what rates. Market-leading supply booking system. |

## 5. Competitive Landscape

### Market Segmentation

The supply teaching technology market can be segmented into three distinct models:

#### 1. Marketplace / Direct (Disruptor Model)
**Examples:** Zen Educate, ClassCover

- Connects schools directly with teachers, bypassing or reducing agency involvement
- Significantly lower fees (Zen Educate charges ~£10-15/day vs agency fees of £40-80/day)
- Teachers earn more, schools pay less
- Platform handles compliance, vetting, and matching
- **Challenge:** Building and maintaining a large enough supply pool; quality assurance without local agency knowledge

**Zen Educate** is the clear category leader with $76.3M in funding, CCS framework accreditation (the only tech-enabled solution on the framework), and aggressive expansion through acquisitions (Aquinas Education in 2024, AK Teaching in January 2026). They operate across 11 regions in England.

#### 2. Agency-Managed Platforms
**Examples:** Teacher Booker (IRIS), Eteach Supply

- Software tools used by schools to manage their relationships with multiple agencies
- Schools set rate caps, preferred suppliers, and cascading rules
- Agencies still source and vet the teachers
- Platform provides transparency and cost control
- **Challenge:** Still reliant on agency margins; does not fundamentally change the cost structure

**Teacher Booker** by IRIS is the market leader in this category, widely adopted by schools and MATs for managing supply bookings across multiple agencies.

#### 3. Hybrid Model
**Examples:** Eteach (increasingly hybrid)

- Combines a school's own supply pool (direct bookings) with agency fallback
- Schools can build internal banks of preferred supply teachers
- Agencies used only when the direct pool cannot fill a vacancy
- **Challenge:** Requires schools to invest time in building and maintaining their own pool

### Competitive Positioning for QuickSupply

QuickSupply's sequential assignment workflow (school -> preferred teachers -> agency cascade) represents a hybrid approach with agency integration at its core. The key differentiators in the market are:

| Factor | Traditional Agencies | Marketplace (Zen Educate) | QuickSupply Opportunity |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Speed of booking | Slow (phone-based) | Fast (app-based) | Real-time sequential cascade |
| Cost to school | High (agency margins) | Low (platform fee) | Competitive (agency-set rates, transparency) |
| Teacher quality signal | Agency reputation | Platform ratings | Integrated review/rating system |
| Compliance management | Agency-handled | Platform-handled | Shared responsibility with audit trail |
| Agency relationship | Required | Eliminated | Preserved but digitised |
| Geographic matching | Manual | Algorithm | Algorithm with real-time availability |

## 6. User Personas

### School Office Manager / Cover Coordinator

**Demographics:** Typically female, 30-55, experienced school administrator.

**Goals:**
- Fill cover vacancies as quickly as possible, especially same-morning requests
- Minimise cost and stay within budget
- Ensure all supply staff are compliant (DBS, safeguarding)
- Maintain the Single Central Record accurately
- Minimise disruption to pupils' learning

**Frustrations:**
- Spending 30-60 minutes on the phone each morning before 8:00 AM trying to find cover
- Juggling multiple agency contacts and rates
- No-shows leaving them scrambling at the last minute
- Paper timesheets and manual invoicing reconciliation
- Getting supply teachers who are not suited to the key stage or school culture

**Tech comfort:** Moderate. Uses school MIS (SIMS, Arbor, Bromcom), email, WhatsApp. Willing to adopt new tools if they demonstrably save time.

### Headteacher / School Business Manager

**Demographics:** Senior leadership, responsible for staffing budgets.

**Goals:**
- Control supply spending (a significant and growing budget line)
- Ensure safeguarding compliance at all times
- Maintain quality of teaching during staff absences
- Build a reliable pool of known, trusted supply teachers
- Meet Ofsted requirements around safer recruitment

**Frustrations:**
- Lack of visibility into supply spend until invoices arrive
- Difficulty tracking which agencies offer best value
- Concern about the quality of unknown supply staff
- AWR compliance tracking across multiple placements
- Temp-to-perm fees when wanting to hire a good supply teacher permanently

**Tech comfort:** Variable. Focused on dashboards, reports, and compliance assurance rather than day-to-day booking.

### Supply Teacher

**Demographics:** Qualified teacher (QTS), range of ages and career stages. May be career supply teachers, returners to teaching, semi-retired, or ECTs seeking experience.

**Goals:**
- Secure regular, well-paid work with minimal gaps
- Work at schools that are well-organised and supportive
- Receive fair pay (ideally at STPCD rates, not discounted agency rates)
- Minimise travel time / work at nearby schools
- Build relationships with preferred schools for repeat bookings

**Frustrations:**
- Being called by multiple agencies at unsociable hours (6:30 AM)
- Uncertainty about whether work will be available day-to-day
- Significant agency margin reducing their take-home pay
- Arriving at schools with no lesson plans, poor behaviour management, or inadequate briefing
- Lack of transparency about pay rates and deductions (especially via umbrella companies)

**Tech comfort:** Generally comfortable with smartphone apps. Already uses WhatsApp, email, and potentially multiple agency portals.

### Agency Consultant

**Demographics:** Recruitment professional, often 25-40, working in a fast-paced, commission-driven environment.

**Goals:**
- Fill as many bookings as possible each day (revenue and commission)
- Maintain a reliable pool of compliant supply teachers
- Build strong relationships with schools to become a preferred supplier
- Ensure compliance to protect the agency's reputation and accreditation
- Reduce no-shows and cancellations

**Frustrations:**
- Frantic mornings making dozens of calls to match supply and demand
- Teachers registered with multiple agencies, leading to availability confusion
- Schools demanding specific teachers or subjects at the last minute
- Managing compliance documentation manually or in outdated CRM systems
- AWR tracking across multiple placements
- Competitive pressure from marketplace disruptors (Zen Educate)

**Tech comfort:** Uses CRM systems, email, phone, WhatsApp extensively. Open to tools that increase fill rates and reduce admin.

## 7. Key Challenges

### 7.1 Teacher Shortage

The fundamental driver of the supply teaching market is structural teacher undersupply:

- Unfilled teacher vacancy rates are **6x higher than pre-pandemic levels**
- 90% of teachers considering leaving cited **high workload** as a factor
- **Pupil behaviour** has become one of the fastest-growing contributors to teacher workload since the pandemic
- Recruitment into Initial Teacher Training remains persistently below target, though 2025/26 saw an 11% improvement (32,000+ trainees)
- Subjects with transferable skills to other industries (maths, physics, computing, languages) face the most severe shortages
- Rural areas and the South of England have the worst supply-demand imbalances

### 7.2 Compliance Burden

The regulatory framework for supply staff is extensive and growing:

- **Enhanced DBS with barred list** checks required before any work begins
- **KCSIE** (updated annually) sets expanding expectations for supply staff vetting and induction
- **Single Central Record** must include all supply staff — creating ongoing data management requirements
- **Right to work** verification with document retention
- **Prohibition order** and **Section 128** checks
- **Overseas checks** for teachers who have lived/worked abroad
- Ensuring supply staff receive **safeguarding briefings** at every new school
- New umbrella company PAYE regulations from April 2026 add further compliance complexity

### 7.3 Quality Assurance

Schools have limited mechanisms to assess supply teacher quality before a booking:

- Agency recommendations are subject to commercial bias
- No standardised rating or review system across the market
- Schools rely on informal networks ("have you used this teacher before?")
- Feedback loops between schools and agencies are slow and inconsistent
- Mismatch between teacher specialisation and school needs (e.g., secondary-trained teacher in a primary EYFS setting)

### 7.4 Last-Minute Bookings

The majority of supply bookings are made on the day of absence:

- Teacher illness is unpredictable — calls come in at 6:30-7:00 AM
- Schools need confirmed cover before 8:30 AM registration
- The matching window is often 60-90 minutes
- Geographic distance must be factored in (a teacher 45 minutes away cannot make it for 8:30 AM if called at 7:45 AM)
- Current phone-based workflows are too slow for this time pressure

### 7.5 Geographic Matching

Effective matching requires real-time geographic intelligence:

- Teachers have maximum travel distance preferences
- Traffic/commute time varies dramatically by time of day and region
- Urban areas have more supply teachers but also more competing demand
- Rural schools may have very limited supply teacher availability
- Schools strongly prefer teachers who know the local area/school

### 7.6 Cost Pressure

Schools face growing pressure to reduce supply spending:

- Agency margins of 15-30% are increasingly scrutinised
- MATs are centralising procurement to negotiate better rates
- DfE-backed CCS framework encourages competitive pricing
- Marketplace disruptors (Zen Educate) are demonstrating that fees can be dramatically reduced
- Schools must balance cost against quality and reliability
- AWR pay parity obligations (after 12 weeks) increase costs for long-term supply placements

### 7.7 Pay Transparency and Fairness

Supply teachers face a persistent pay gap compared to permanent staff:

- Daily rates for qualified supply teachers range from **£153-233/day outside London** and **£190-270/day in London** (2025 rates)
- Agency margins mean teachers often receive significantly less than what schools pay
- There is no national pay framework for agency-employed supply teachers — agencies set their own rates
- Umbrella company deductions (employer's NIC, holiday pay accrual, pension, margin) further reduce take-home pay
- AWR pay parity only applies after 12 weeks, and agencies may restructure placements to avoid triggering it

## 8. QuickSupply Opportunity Summary

The supply teaching market presents a clear opportunity for a platform that:

1. **Preserves agency relationships** while digitising them — unlike Zen Educate's disintermediation approach, QuickSupply can position as a tool that makes agencies more efficient rather than replacing them
2. **Enables real-time sequential assignment** — school posts need, system checks preferred/known teachers first, then cascades to agency pools, all within the critical 60-90 minute morning window
3. **Centralises compliance** — digital DBS tracking, automated safeguarding briefing acknowledgement, SCR-ready data exports
4. **Builds quality signals** — integrated review and rating system (school reviews of teachers, teacher reviews of schools) creates a feedback loop currently absent from the market
5. **Provides cost transparency** — schools see exactly what they are paying and agencies see fill rates, reducing the information asymmetry
6. **Handles geographic intelligence** — real-time availability matched with travel time to ensure feasible same-morning placements
7. **Targets primary education** — a focused vertical where class teacher cover is simpler (single class, generalist teaching) than secondary subject-specialist matching

The £757M annual market with 7.5% year-on-year growth, combined with persistent teacher shortages and increasing compliance requirements, creates strong demand for a modern scheduling and assignment platform.
